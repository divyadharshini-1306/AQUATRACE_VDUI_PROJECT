# Review 2: Member 4 paper and SWOT

## Paper
Segel, E. and Heer, J. "Narrative Visualization: Telling Stories with Data."
IEEE Trans. Visualization and Computer Graphics (InfoVis 2010). DOI 10.1109/TVCG.2010.179
Won the IEEE InfoVis 10-Year Test of Time Award.

- What it does: analyses 58 narrative visualizations; defines 7 genres (magazine style, annotated chart, partitioned poster, flow chart, comic strip, slide show, video)
- Design space: genre + visual narrative tactics + narrative structure tactics
- Key idea: balance of author-driven vs reader-driven; hybrid structures Martini Glass, Interactive Slideshow, Drill-Down Story
- Finding: tacit tutorials, stimulating default views and summaries are under-used
- Limitation: design-focused, little on reader experience; mostly journalism; no accessibility coverage

## How AquaTrace applies it
- Pages 1-6 as an interactive slideshow with a martini-glass ending (slicers = free exploration)
- Consistent visual platform via shared theme JSON
- Fixed risk-tier colours (semantic consistency), colour-blind-safe palette
- Insight-sentence titles and annotations; Page 6 = synthesis
- Our usability test and accessibility checklist address the paper's reader-experience and accessibility gaps

## SWOT (project-level DRAFT: align with Divya)
Strengths: cooling system explains ~68% of WUE variance; EDA and model cross-validated; consistent accessible design; clear narrative arc
Weaknesses: operational values are modelled estimates, not measured; year trend is stylised; limited to 5 visuals in the .pbix
Opportunities: same pipeline can take real disclosed facility data; decision-support tool for sustainability officers; case stories from Epoch AI data
Threats: viewers may read modelled estimates as real measurements; inconsistent operator disclosure; misleading-visual risk
